# My role

I conceived and own ContextLab. I did not treat the work as a coding exercise. I led the research,
designed the system, owned the product decisions, directed the agents, and made every final human
decision.

## Research lead

I defined the central question: when should an enterprise AI system retrieve more, remember more,
search more, and when should it stay simple?

I chose the controlled comparison, the NovaLearn synthetic setting, the frozen variables, the
sealed-evaluation boundary, the promotion criteria, and the limits on public claims. I decided that
a failed gate must remain a result and cannot be cleaned away after the fact.

## Systems architect

I designed the separation between corpus and events, strategy adapters, candidate evidence, context
packs, the provider gateway, review gates, and the evidence viewer. I required every displayed metric
to keep its artifact path, JSON pointer, source-run identity, and SHA-256.

I also set the failure behavior. Protected data stays outside the system under test. Unknown sealed
fields fail closed. Provider failures remain failures. A changed artifact loses its old approval.

## Product owner

I decided what the public experience must explain in five minutes and what the detailed laboratory
must preserve for technical inspection. I kept the guided Story and the analytical viewer in one
React and Vite product.

I made the no-ship decisions. G2 did not promote an advanced retriever. G3 did not promote a memory
policy. F3 and F5 remain accepted-negative demonstrations. Added machinery did not become product
architecture because it looked interesting.

## Agent orchestrator

I divided implementation and review into bounded tasks with named inputs, file ownership, stop
conditions, and required verification. Agents returned code, tests, evidence projections, or review
findings for inspection. I reconciled their work against the research contract and current
repository state.

No agent could approve its own output. A proposal agent could not act as its reviewer. An AI review
could pass a technical workspace, but it could not create human approval.

## Final human authority

I audited the evidence and made every final human decision. I am the sole human reviewer. Public
material must state that limit whenever it describes the work as human-audited.

My approvals bind exact technical or pending-gate hashes. A later byte change does not inherit an
earlier approval. This is why the historical G4 approval does not cover later viewer work, and why
the portfolio release needs its own final packet.

## Responsibility boundary

I am accountable for:

- the research questions and claim scope;
- the experimental controls and stop rules;
- the architecture and product direction;
- the agent task boundaries and review method;
- the evidence audit and final gate decisions;
- the public narrative and release decision.

Agents are accountable for the bounded work recorded in their task contracts. Model providers are
external dependencies. AI reviewers are independent technical reviewers within the stated process.
None of those roles transfers project ownership or final authority away from me.
